# Pool: standards — loading/conditioning protocols for electret media and their evidence base

(Working file; entries appended as sources are read. "VERIFIED" = read in primary document; "UNVERIFIED" = secondary/snippet only.)

## S1. ASHRAE 52.2 Informative Appendix J (KCl conditioning, MERV-A) — procedure as published

Source: ANSI/ASHRAE Addendum b to ANSI/ASHRAE Standard 52.2-2007 (2008), in "52.2-2007 Supplement" — https://www.ashrae.org/File%20Library/Technical%20Resources/Standards%20and%20Guidelines/Standards%20Addenda/52_2_2007_Supplement_FINAL.pdf (pp. 4-11). VERIFIED (read full text).

- Origin stated in foreword: RP-1190 ("Develop a New Loading Dust and Dust Loading Procedures for the ASHRAE Filter Test Standards 52.1 and 52.2") and RP-1189 ("Investigation of Mechanisms and Operating Environments that Impact the Filtration Efficiency of Charged Air Filtration Media") "and subsequent committee action". Revised A2.3: method "has been demonstrated in limited cases to more closely predict filter performance in field use." Foreword quote of 52.2-1999: the initial conditioning step (first 30 g dust load) "may affect the efficiency of the filter but not as much as would be observed in actual service."
- Status: informative, not ANSI approved (no third public review). J1: "representative of the best available knowledge of real life filter efficiency degradation at the time of the publication ... filters may or may not degrade to the conditioned efficiencies".
- Aerosol (J3.2, J8.1): Laskin nozzle(s), 20-60 psig, aqueous KCl 1.00 g/L (0.1 %), reagent grade, NOT required to be charge-neutralised. Solid phase. Size distribution not specified directly; constrained only by J5.1.5/J10.7: ratio of CPC count (CPC d50 ≤ 0.02 µm) to OPC count >0.3 µm must be > 20,000 — i.e. essentially all particles ultrafine.
- Concentration (J10.4): ≤ 1.0 × 10^6 cm^-3 in duct.
- Dose metric = CT (CPC number concentration × time). Increments ≥ 6.4 × 10^7 particles·cm^-3·min (J10.5), PSE (0.3-10 µm, OPC, KCl test aerosol) after each increment. Stop when (a) no further "significant drop" (≥ 2 percentage points in ≥ 2 adjacent size ranges vs minima so far) or (b) cumulative CT = 1.2 × 10^9 particles·cm^-3·min. Note: large-media-area filters (300 mm deep, bags) "may require" the full 1.2e9.
- Airflow = same as test airflow (typically 0.944 m³/s for 610×610 mm). RH < 65 % throughout, and storage < 65 % RH (to prevent KCl deliquescence) (J10.8).
- NO MASS is specified anywhere in the procedure. Mass must be inferred from CT × flow × mean particle mass (see derived estimate below once size distribution is sourced).
- Then the normal ASHRAE dust loading, minus the 30 g conditioning dust load; MERV-A from minimum composite PSE.

## S2. Lehtimäki 1996, Nordtest NT TR 320 "Development of test methods for electret filters" (VTT) — the origin of the IPA test and the "minimum efficiency" idea later in EN 779:2012 / ISO 16890-4. VERIFIED (scanned PDF, OCR'd + figures read).

Source: M. Lehtimäki, NT Techn Report 320, Nordtest project 1164-94, approved 1996-03, 57 pp. Page: https://www.nordtest.info/wp/1996/03/06/development-of-test-methods-for-electret-filters-nt-tr-320/ ; PDF: http://nordtest.info/images/documents/nt-technical-reports/NT%20TR%20320_Development%20of%20test%20methods%20for%20electret%20filters_Nordtest%20Technical%20Report.pdf

Media (Table 2.1, p.6): two commercial EU7 electret bag filters. "A": electret layer of coarse fibres ~15-20 µm; full size 600x600, 4 bags, 2.8 m² media, 0.94 m³/s, media velocity 0.33 m/s; half-size 2 bags (~1.4 m²), 0.47 m³/s. "B": flat fibres ~5 x 15 µm, mostly mechanical; full 8 bags, 5.6 m², 1.18 m³/s, 0.21 m/s; half 4 bags, 2.8 m². Polymer, basis weight, charging method NOT given. Material tests on 150 mm diameter samples cut from the bags, at the nominal media velocity (33 cm/s A, 21 cm/s B).
Efficiency test: polydisperse KCl, Kr-85 neutralised, LAS-X OPC (PSL-equivalent size), COUNT-based; reported at 0.4 µm.
Loading metric: mass FED per m² media (gravimetric upstream conc × v_media × t) — explicitly "does not correspond to the amount of particles collected" (p.4). Deposited mass is lower.

Readings below are read off figures (precision about ±2-3 percentage points E). E = efficiency at 0.4 µm.

S2a. IPA (immersion in technical-grade isopropanol, 24 h drying), Figs 4.1-4.2 (p.14): A 90% -> ~18%; B ~78% -> ~42% (B samples very inhomogeneous: clean E(0.4) ranged ~57-78% between samples, Figs 2.5, 3.9, 4.2, 4.7). IPA did not change Δp.

S2b. Diesel fume (Yanmar 0.199 L single-cylinder engine at 2700 rpm, ~850 W load; mass size distribution Fig 4.3, mostly < ~0.5 µm aerodynamic), material A at 33 cm/s, Fig 4.5 (p.16):
  fed g/m²:  0 -> 1.5 -> 3.1 -> 5.3 -> 7.4 -> 10.6
  E(0.4) %:  89.7 -> 67.4 -> 53.9 -> 21.3 -> 19.7 -> 20.0
  -lnP retained: 1 -> 0.49 -> 0.34 -> 0.11 -> 0.10 -> 0.10
  fraction of electrostatic part lost (vs IPA E 18%, -lnP 0.198): 0.56 at 1.5 g/m²; 0.72 at 3.1; 0.98 at 5.3.
  Text p.17: "the amount of diesel fume necessary to remove the electrostatic removal mechanism is approximately 6 g/m2 (filter "A") and 1 g/m2 (filter "B"). It is worth mentioning that these loading levels did not cause any increase in the pressure drop".
  Material B at 21 cm/s, Fig 4.7 (p.17): fed 0 -> 0.94 -> 2.0 -> 3.4 -> 4.8 -> 6.9 g/m²; E 56.9 -> 28.6 -> 29.3 -> 30.6 -> 32.3 -> 35.7 %. -lnP retained 0.40 at 0.94 g/m² (then slow mechanical recovery).

S2c. Side-stream cigarette smoke (freely burning, vertical), Fig 4.10 (A, p.19) and 4.12 (B, p.20):
  A: fed 0 -> 0.9 -> 2.7 -> 4.4 -> 8.0 -> 12.0 g/m²; E 91.0 -> 88.1 -> 76.9 -> 63.9 -> 41.4 -> 24.1 %; -lnP retained 1 -> 0.88 -> 0.61 -> 0.42 -> 0.22 -> 0.11; electrostatic part lost 0.13 / 0.42 / 0.63 / 0.85 / 0.96.
  B: fed 0 -> 0.6 -> 1.7 -> 2.9 -> 5.2 -> 7.5 g/m²; E 69.0 -> 48.7 -> 41.5 -> 40.8 -> 41.6 -> 42.6 %.
  Text p.17: smoke needed to remove the electrostatic effect "approximately 12 g/m2 (filter "A") and 2 g/m2 (filter "B")", "high enough to cause a slight increase in the pressure drop".
  => per gram FED, diesel soot was ~2x as damaging as side-stream smoke (A: 50 % of electrostatic -lnP gone at ~1.3 g/m² diesel vs ~3.3 g/m² smoke).

S2d. Ambient air, 24 days, material samples (Figs 3.8-3.9, p.12): A 4.4 g/m² fed -> E(0.4) ~90 -> ~56 % (-lnP retained 0.36; ~70 % of electrostatic part lost vs IPA). B 2.8 g/m² fed -> ~58 -> ~44 %. Implied mean ambient conc 4.4/(0.33 m/s x 24 d) = 6.4 µg/m³ (same for B). Suburban Tampere.

S2e. Field, 4 months suburban Tampere (Dec start), half-size bag filters at 0.5 m³/s behind an EU3 prefilter, whole unit tested (Figs 3.6-3.7, p.11; text p.10): ambient ~10 µg/m³ -> ~50 g fed per unit = "36 g/m2 (filter A) and 18 g/m2 (filter B)" (fed to the unit upstream of the EU3 prefilter; electret received less). E(0.4) unit: A ~88 -> ~24 %; B ~77 -> ~45 %. Both ended close to their IPA-treated level (A ~18 %, B ~42 %; different samples) -> roughly 95 % of the electrostatic contribution gone after 4 months.

S2f. ASHRAE 52-76 dust (full 600x600 bag filters, 1.0 m³/s, Holmberg 1995; Figs 3.2-3.5): A up to 390 g fed (= 139 g/m²) E(0.4) "remains essentially constant"; B up to 305 g (54 g/m²) increases. PTI Fine (Arizona-type) dust on material: A slight dip then rise; B rises (Figs 5.8-5.11). 
S2g. Bimodal PTI dust + diesel mixtures (Figs 5.32-5.35, x-axis µg/m³·yr fed per unit flow; convert with v_media x 3.156e7 s: x10.4 g/m² per µg/m³·yr for A, x6.6 for B). A minima: PTI-only ~79 % (from ~88); 10 % diesel ~72 %; 15 % diesel ~50 %; 25 % diesel ~30 % at ~4.5 µg/m³·yr (~47 g/m² fed, ~12 g/m² of it diesel); 35 % diesel ~26 % at ~2.2 µg/m³·yr (~23 g/m² fed, ~8 g/m² diesel). Text: penetration at 0.4 µm rose "as much as by a factor of 6 in the case of filter A and by a factor of 2 in the case of filter B". Coarse dust co-loading offsets the electret loss through mechanical cake build-up; the depth of the minimum scales with the fine combustion fraction.

Draft Nordtest method (Appendix, pp.47-57): (i) IPA test: immerse in technical-grade IPA, dry 24 h flat in fume cupboard, re-measure E(0.4); report X = (E0 - E1)/E0. (ii) "Combustion aerosol test": diesel fume in steps of 5 g/m² fed; continue past 15 g/m² until efficiency rises; minimum used. Conclusion p.44: "More reasonable approach to describe the performance of a filter is to specify the minimum level of the efficiency ... Filter loading with a test dust, e.g. ASHRAE test dust, should be used just to determine the filter pressure drop". Cites SINTEF 1995 field report "Langtidstesting av filter i virkelig miljø" (STF11 A95027) and Gustavsson 1993 (Energi & Miljö) as field evidence — not found online yet.
QF (my arithmetic, 0.4 µm, count-based, using clean-sample Δp from Fig 5.8/5.13 legends: A ≈ 76-89 Pa at 33 cm/s; B ≈ 134-141 Pa at 21 cm/s; different samples from the IPA ones, so ±30 %): A clean ≈ 2.30/0.080 ≈ 29 kPa⁻¹ @33 cm/s, A after IPA ≈ 0.20/0.080 ≈ 2.5 kPa⁻¹; B clean ≈ 1.5/0.135 ≈ 11 kPa⁻¹ @21 cm/s (E 78 %), B after IPA ≈ 0.54/0.135 ≈ 4 kPa⁻¹.

## S3. Evidence base behind ASHRAE Appendix J

S3a. RP-1189 (VTT; Lehtimäki M., Säämänen A., 2005) "Investigation of Mechanisms and Operating Environments that Impact the Filtration Efficiency of Charged Air Filtration Media", final report to ASHRAE in 2 phases (contaminant/literature review; lab tests of aerosol type, operating conditions, particle size and resistivity + field tests of full-scale filters and media at urban and suburban sites). WALLED - ASHRAE store: https://store.accuristech.com/standards/rp-1189-investigation-of-mechanisms-and-operating-environments-that-impact-the-filtration-efficiency-of-charged-air-filtration-media?product_id=1711879 (abstract read at https://www.n-standards.com/standards/RP-1189-Investigation-of-Mechanisms-and-Operating-Environments-that-Impact-the-Filtration-Efficiency-of-Charged-Air-Filtration-Media/). Not found published elsewhere. This is THE ASHRAE dataset on which aerosols kill the charge, at what mass, in the field - the top walled item.

S3b. RP-1190 (Hanley & Owen, RTI, 2003), new loading dust - WALLED; cited by 52.2 Addendum b as the other origin of App J.

S3c. Raynor & Chae 2003, "Dust loading on electrostatically charged filters in a standard test and a real HVAC system", Filtration+Separation 40(2):35-39. https://stacks.cdc.gov/view/cdc/188536 VERIFIED (full text). Polyolefin (PE/PP meltblown) charged vs glass, both nominal 90-95 % dust-spot, 0.61 x 0.61 m filters, 15 pleats 28 cm deep x 54 cm tall (my arithmetic: ~4.5 m² media per filter; 1968 cfm = 0.93 m³/s -> ~21 cm/s media velocity), 100 % outdoor air behind prefilters that took >90 % at 3 µm, 19 weeks. Charged filters lost up to 40 % (efficiency points), minimum after a 15-20 % Δp rise, then rose; the 52.2-1999 dust test on the same products INCREASED efficiency. No mass data. Authors attribute the gap to sub-µm combustion-derived mass in the field vs > 1 µm mineral test dust. (Field pool has the JOEH 2004 version.)

S3d. RP-1360 (Owen, Pope & Hanley, RTI), "How do pressure drop, efficiency, weight gain, and loaded dust composition change throughout filter lifetime?" ASHRAE Transactions 120(1):366-381 (2014). WALLED: https://www.techstreet.com/ashrae/standards/ny-14-032-how-do-pressure-drop-efficiency-weight-gain-and-loaded-dust-composition-change-throughout-filter-lifetime-rp-1360?product_id=1876220 . Abstract (RTI page https://www.rti.org/publication/how-do-pressure-drop-efficiency-weight-gain-and-loaded-dust-composition-change-0): App J "was needed to bracket the minimum in-situ efficiency of the electret filter"; "Reporting values calculated from the minimum efficiency curve are much more relevant to in-situ performance." The report holds filter WEIGHT GAIN vs efficiency for a charged 1-inch MERV 11 - exactly the quantity Matt wants - but the numbers are not public.
  Secondary table (UNVERIFIED; Tex-Air Filters / J. Rosenthal 2014, a commercial critic: https://www.texairfilters.com/ashrae-research-project-report-on-air-filters-raises-more-questions-than-it-answers/), 1-inch electret "MERV 11" residential filter, E1 (0.3-1 µm) / E2 / E3 %: new (52.2) 37/80/93 (MERV 12); after App J 13/51/74 (MERV 8-A); in-situ MN 2 mo 14/55/72; MN 4 mo 21/74/95; NC 1.5 mo 15/66/86; NC 3.5 mo 12/61/91; GA 1.5 mo 13/52/71; GA 5 mo 35/74/87. So field E1 fell to about the App J level (12-15 %) within 1.5-3.5 months in 3 of 4 cities, sometimes recovering later with cake. -lnP(E1) retained: 0.46 -> ~0.14-0.16 = ~0.3-0.35 of initial. No weight gains in the secondary source.

S3e. RP-1649 (Li & Siegel, U Toronto; STBE 2020, "Laboratory performance of new and used residential HVAC filters - comparison to field results (RP-1649)"): 21 Toronto homes, 4 filter types, 3-month deployments, new filters tested per App J. ASHRAE Journal summary (https://www.ashrae.org/news/ashraejournal/ashrae-rp-1649-comparing-laboratory-performance-and-in-situ-performance-of-residential-hvac-filters): lab results "generally overestimate in-situ efficiency"; 52.2 dust loading + conditioning gives "a reasonable range". Full text WALLED (tandfonline). No numbers obtained.

S3f. A real App J run - Camfil Durafil ES2 M14 (wet-laid FIBREGLASS V-bed, i.e. NOT electret; 192 ft² = 17.8 m² media; 1970 cfm), test report 2016: avg conditioning conc 5.76e5 cm⁻³, 248 min, cumulative CT 1.43e8 cm⁻³·min, > 0.3 µm count 24,833 ft⁻³ (0.88 cm⁻³), small:large ratio 656,602; MERV 14 -> MERV-A 14A (E1 78 -> 75 %). https://rtf.nwcouncil.org/media/filer_public/14/80/14808842-d5d5-4b33-8298-6a2eed9f6db3/Test_Report_-_Durafil_ES2.pdf VERIFIED. Useful as (i) a control (non-electret barely moves) and (ii) the only real CT/size-ratio numbers found.

S3g. MY ESTIMATE (not from any source) of the KCl mass in App J. Dose = CT x Q x mean particle mass. Using the measured ratio (fraction > 0.3 µm = 1.5e-6) and a lognormal assumption: GSD 1.6 -> CMD ≈ 33 nm (volume-mean ≈ 46 nm); GSD 1.8 -> CMD ≈ 19 nm (v-mean ≈ 32 nm); GSD 2.0 -> CMD ≈ 12 nm (v-mean ≈ 24 nm). KCl density 1.98 g/cm³ -> 1.4e-17 to 1.0e-16 g per particle. Durafil run: N = 1.43e8 x 5.58e7 cm³/min = 8.0e15 -> 0.1-0.8 g KCl challenged = 6-45 mg/m² over its 17.8 m². Maximum CT 1.2e9 at 0.944 m³/s: N = 6.8e16 -> about 1-7 g KCl challenged per filter, i.e. about 1-7 g/m² challenged for a ~1 m²-media 1-inch pleat, about 0.06-0.4 g/m² for a 17.8 m² V-bank. Mass concentration at 5.8e5 cm⁻³ ≈ 8-60 µg/m³ (order of ambient PM2.5, but all ultrafine). Implication: the App J dose is fixed per unit FLOW (number concentration x time), so dose per m² media scales inversely with media area - consistent with the J10.5 note that large-media filters need the full CT. CT_max = 1.2e9 cm⁻³·min is ~83 days at 1e4 cm⁻³ (typical urban number concentration) - my arithmetic; the standard gives no rationale for the number.

## S4. EN 779:2002/2012 and ISO 16890-4 (IPA discharge)

- Lineage, from Eurovent REC 4/20 (May 2012), "Recommendation concerning classification of air filters" (VERIFIED, https://www.eurovent.eu/wp-content/uploads/publications-files/eurovent-rec-4-20-recommendation-concerning-classification-of-air-filters-2012-en.pdf): EN 779:2002 Annex A "discharging method was an adoption of the NT VVS 117 method for determination of the discharged efficiency of electrostatically charged air filter media" (= the Nordtest method drafted in NT TR 320, S2). EN 779:2002 allowed several treatments, mostly liquid IPA (media samples) or diesel fume (whole filters, Eurovent Recommendation 18). EN 779:2012: IPA on media only; >= 3 samples, >= 600 cm² total; "minimum efficiency" = lowest of initial, discharged and lowest-during-dust-loading efficiency at 0.4 µm; F7 >= 35 %, F8 >= 55 %, F9 >= 70 % (Table 1); M5/M6 have no minimum. Table 1 note: "Loss of media charge or shedding of particles or fibres can also adversely affect efficiency." Diesel-fume results accepted during transition.
- EN 779:2012 immersion "at least 2 min" then dry; ISO 16890-4:2016/2022 "conditioning of the assembled filters in IPA-saturated air for 24 h and subsequent storage in ambient air for 30 min" (Kerner et al. 2021, Atmosphere 12:65, §2.3; open copy https://kluedo.ub.rptu.de/files/6247/atmosphere-12-00065-v2.pdf VERIFIED; NAFA also says 24 h).
- ISO 16890-4:2022 text (iTeh preview, VERIFIED https://cdn.standards.iteh.ai/samples/79593/f7155fe97b944e90b39fff1f37b1dedd/ISO-16890-4-2022.pdf): full 610 x 610 element, IPA >= 99.5 %, cabinet 0.45-0.65 m³. §5.1: "Exposure to some types of challenges, such as combustion particles, fine particles or oil mist in service can affect the action of these electric charges so that the initial efficiency can drop substantially after an initial period of service. This drop in the fractional efficiency can be reduced by a slight increase in mechanical efficiency from the collection of particles in the filtration media. The amount of the drop and the amount of the increase can vary by filter type, service location and atmospheric air conditions." Also: "It should not be assumed that the measured conditioned ("discharged") efficiency always represents real life behaviour." Not applicable if IPA changes Δp > 10 % and >= 10 Pa. ePMx uses the MEAN of initial and conditioned efficiency per size bin.
- NAFA (industry) explainer "ISO 16890", Jan 2026 (https://www.nafahq.org/assets/pdf/ISO+16890+January+2026): "Since the IPA conditioning is expected to overpredict the loss in efficiency in real use for charged filters, the average of the two values in each size range is then calculated as representative of the filter's efficiency in use"; "The Appendix J efficiencies may be comparable to the average efficiencies for the clean and IPA conditioning steps in ISO 16890." Committee-consensus claim; I found no published dataset behind the 50/50 weighting.
- Discharge-method science (not loading): saturated vapour discharges more completely than liquid for low-permeability media (Kerner 2021 Table 4; Building & Environment 2018 "Evaluation of different discharging methods on HVAC electret filter media": residual 2 µC/m² liquid vs 0 vapour - secondary).
- Field-vs-IPA evidence actually found: only NT TR 320 (S2e: two EU7 bag filters at about their IPA level after 4 months, ~18-37 g/m² ambient fed) and RP-1360 (S3d: App J level reached in 1.5-3.5 months). CETIAT/Eurovent intercomparisons not found online.

## S5. NIOSH 42 CFR 84 (N/R/P)

- Text, 42 CFR 84.174 (eCFR, VERIFIED https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-42/chapter-I/subchapter-G/part-84/subpart-K): 85 L/min single filter (42.5 L/min each of a pair); N: NaCl, CMD 0.075 ± 0.020 µm, GSD <= 1.86, 25 °C, 30 ± 10 % RH, Boltzmann-neutralised, <= 200 mg/m³, after 25 h at 85 % RH / 38 °C preconditioning; R/P: neat cold-nebulised DOP, CMD 0.185 ± 0.020 µm, GSD <= 1.60, <= 200 mg/m³; photometric (forward light scattering) -> mass-weighted, not count. "The test will continue until minimum efficiency is achieved or until an aerosol mass of at least 200 ± 5 mg has contacted the filter. For P-series filters, if the filter efficiency is decreasing when the 200 ± 5 mg challenge point is reached, the test will be continued until there is no further decrease in efficiency." Same for PAPR100-N/P (84.180).
- Mass per area: 200 mg "contacted" (challenged, not deposited) on an N95 FFR of ~150 cm² (3M 1860, stated in arXiv 2006.02470 with ~9.3 cm/s at 85 L/min; one source, approximate) ≈ 13 g/m² challenged, ≈ 12-13 g/m² deposited at >= 95 %. Paired cartridges: 200 mg each at 42.5 L/min.
- Rationale (1995 final rule preamble, 60 FR 30336, VERIFIED https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-1995-06-08/html/95-13287.htm): "NIOSH selected the test criteria to be a combination of worst-case and very severe test conditions"; "NIOSH is aware that few data are available to assess the performance of these respirators in workplace settings over an extended period of time"; commenters' "workplace studies were submitted to support the conclusion that, because of the highly degrading nature of DOP and the proposed high loading level, the proposed test were many times more severe than conditions realistically encountered by workers"; the tests "are not designed to simulate loading of the filter at the worksite." Opposing commenters: workplace contaminants "can cause the beneficial filtering effects of the charge on electrostatic filters to become partially or totally ineffective without indication to the wearer". 1994 proposal (59 FR 26850, https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-1994-05-24/html/94-11879.htm): 200 ± 5 mg non-powered, 2000 ± 50 mg PAPR; 85 L/min "representative of a high work rate"; loading chosen to "ensure that a sufficient number of particulates are applied". No exposure-based derivation of 200 mg in either preamble.
- NIOSH User Notice, 2 May 1997 (https://archive.cdc.gov/www_cdc_gov/niosh/npptl/usernotices/notices/run-050297.html): NIOSH lab work found P100 filters after long oil loading fell to efficiencies well below what P95 filters must meet, without a Δp rise, varying widely by model -> manufacturers set P-series time limits; N-series in dirty workplaces: use > 8 h needs evidence that efficiency holds or that total filter loading stays below 200 mg; R-series: single shift/8 h with oil.
- 3M Technical Data Bulletin #137 (Dec 1997, "Understanding P-series particulate filters"; copy https://cdn.shopify.com/s/files/1/0597/6349/1008/files/TDB137.pdf VERIFIED): NIOSH DOP at 100 mg/m³ is "50 to 100 times greater than oil mist concentrations typically found in the workplace"; metal-working-fluid survey mean 0.7 mg/m³ (MMAD 3.6-8.2 µm); at 2 mg/m³ and ~10 m³ breathed per 8 h day, "it would take 10 days of continuous use to reach 200 mg"; 3M rule for oil-only environments: discard after 40 h or 30 days; "all P-series filters, both electrostatic and mechanical filters, will eventually experience a decrease in filter efficiency when subjected to DOP testing beyond the current NIOSH loading requirements"; "Neither 3M nor NIOSH has been able to locate an oil-only environment."
- NIOSH follow-up data (liquid pool territory): Rengasamy et al. 2013, J Eng Fibers Fabrics 8(3):62 (https://stacks.cdc.gov/view/cdc/195150): two N95 FFR models exceeded 5 % DOP penetration at > 100 mg; P100s held < 0.03 % to 200 mg, but one P100 model pre-loaded with only 10-50 mg DOP exceeded 0.03 % on subsequent NaCl tests. Moyer & Bergman 2000 AOEH 15:600 (intermittent NaCl on N95) - not read (fetch refused by tool).

## S6. EN 143 / EN 149 / EN 13274-7

- EN 149:2001+A1:2009 and EN 143:2000/A1:2006 use the EN 13274-7 penetration methods (NaCl by flame photometry; paraffin oil by light scattering) at 95 L/min; the "exposure test" loads 120 mg of each aerosol and the maximum penetration during loading must stay within the class limit (FFP1/2/3: 20/6/1 %). Re-usable devices add a storage test. EN 13274-7:2019 TOC (VERIFIED, preview https://normy.normoff.gov.sk/norma/129542/nahlad): §5.4 Exposure test, §5.5 Storage test (2019 deleted the reference to EN 13274-5 in the storage test). The 120 mg and the limits are VERIFIED only via accredited-lab test reports/datasheets (secondary); aerosol concentrations, sizes and storage duration NOT verified (standard paywalled).
- Mass per area (my arithmetic): 120 mg on a ~150-200 cm² FFP2 ≈ 6-8 g/m², about 0.6x the NIOSH load, at a slightly higher flow (95 vs 85 L/min).
- No rationale document for 120 mg found.

## S7. GB/T 18801-2015 CCM (particles) and 3M's media-level version

- GB/T 18801-2015 Annex D (English translation preview, VERIFIED https://www.chinesestandard.net/PDF-EN/GBT18801-2015EN-P23P-H19316H-050986.pdf): accelerated test in a 3 m³ chamber, only for purifiers with CADR >= 60 m³/h; pollutant = cigarette smoke, "tar content ... 8 mg" (e.g. HongTaShan classic 150); load 50 cigarettes per step, run the purifier until < 0.035 mg/m³, re-measure CADR after each 50; "When the measured clean air delivery rate is less than or equal to 50% of the initial value, the test is ended." CCM = cumulative particulate mass removed (mg) at that point. Grades (secondary, IQAir, UNVERIFIED): P1 3,000-5,000 mg; P2 5,000-8,000; P3 8,000-12,000; P4 >= 12,000. Annex G converts CCM to service life assuming a 35 µg/m³ indoor target, 0.6 h⁻¹ ventilation, 0.8 penetration, 12 h/day.
- 3M "Media CCM" test (US 12,409,401 B2, examples; image PDF https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/12409401, OCR'd, VERIFIED): 11.4 cm diameter exposed media in a recirculating chamber; cigarettes (filter removed; IR4F/IR6F or Camel) burned one at a time until the smoke clears; capture efficiency by TSI 8130 NaCl at 85 L/min (14 cm/s); endpoint = capture efficiency at 50 % of its initial value (2nd-order fit); result in cigarettes per m² media. Reference: Filtrete room-purifier HEPA-type web (FAPF-F2-H / FAPF-A2-4 type), meltblown PP electret with charging additive, EFD ≈ 4.9 µm, 42 g/m², solidity 8.1 %: Media CCM ≈ 520 cigarettes/m². Charged PMP-sheath spunbond prefilters alone (EFD 22-27 µm, 76-102 g/m²): 40-70 cig/m²; prefilter + reference web: 759-933 cig/m² (synergy claimed; the prefilter must be charged). Smoke mass per cigarette not given in this patent (method detail is in WO2021/028851 - patent pool).

## S11. NT VVS 117 (Nordtest method, approved Nov 1997) - the method EN 779:2002 Annex A adopted. VERIFIED: http://nordtest.info/images/documents/nt-methods/polymers/NT%20vvs%20117_Electret%20filters_Determination%20of%20the%20electrostatic%20enhancement%20factor%20of%20filter%20media_Nordtest%20Method.pdf
- IPA test: >=5 media samples (150 mm diameter), immersed in 100 % (reagent or technical grade) isopropanol, dried 24 h flat in a fume cupboard, E(0.4 µm) re-measured (Eurovent 4/9 OPC, DEHS or latex; KCl only if shown equivalent); index X = 100(E0 - E1)/E0 at 0.4 µm.
- Combustion aerosol test: diesel fume, filter exposure m_A = v_A x sum(c_m,i x t_i) (mass FED per m² media), three steps of 5 g/m² (to 15 g/m²), continue in 5 g/m² steps until efficiency rises; minimum used for X. Gravimetric concentration, cascade impactor sizing down to 0.05 µm.
- So the European standards' original 'electret worst case' was explicitly a mass-per-media-area dose of fine combustion aerosol (>= 15 g/m² diesel fed), calibrated on NT TR 320 where 1-6 g/m² diesel removed the electrostatic effect.
